# Data-quality detector: evaluation

Generated by `node scripts/quality/evaluate.ts` from the committed data. Seeded, so the same data gives the same report. Nothing in `data/` is changed.

Scope: 39 FX series against SGD (BND is left out: it is a constant 1:1 peg), 278,056 days, 1082 series-years.

## What it checks

Per currency and day, from its daily log return `r` (SGD per unit):

- **Robust z-score** of `r` against the trailing 250 returns (median and MAD, at least 15 of them; the day itself is never in its own baseline).
- **Peer check:** the same for `r` minus the median move of the other currencies in its region (`scripts/quality/peers.ts`) that day. Moves every SGD cross shares, such as the SGD itself or the euro leg of the ECB crosses, cancel out.
- **Reversal:** whether the next day takes the move back, leaving at most a fifth of it. A bad print comes back; a crisis whipsaw rarely comes all the way back.
- **Stale runs:** the same value on consecutive days.
- **Unit errors:** a day about 10, 100 or 1000 times (or a fraction of) the day before; **inversions:** a day about 1 / the day before.
- **Isolation forest** (100 trees, 256-day samples, seed 20261116) over the absolute z-score, the absolute peer z-score and the reversal, for unusual combinations the rules miss.

Flags, one per series and day:

| Kind     | Severity | Rule (proposed thresholds)                                                      |
| -------- | -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| unit     | severe   | ratio to the day before within 0.1 (in log terms) of 10^k, k = ±1, ±2, ±3       |
| inverted | severe   | value within 0.05 (log) of 1 / the day before, and a move of at least 0.2 (log) |
| spike    | soft     |                                                                                 | z   | ≥ 6 and  | peer z | ≥ 6, taken back the next day |
| move     | soft     |                                                                                 | z   | ≥ 20 and | peer z | ≥ 20, taken back or not      |
| stale    | soft     | 3 or more equal values in a row                                                 |
| outlier  | soft     | forest score ≥ 0.78, when no rule above applies                                 |

## (c) Synthetic errors

Each row puts one kind of error into 300 randomly chosen days (seed 20261116) that the detector leaves alone in the real data, one at a time, and counts the days it flags. Spikes go up or down at random and are right again the next day; unit errors and inversions last one day; stale runs repeat the previous value. "On arrival" is the night the bad day comes in, before the next day is known (for a stale run, the night it reaches 3 equal values).

## Reading the results

- **Severe flags fire 0 times on the real history** and catch 100.0% of synthetic unit errors and 97.0% of inversions on the night they arrive. They are safe to make blocking.
- **Soft flags are mostly real events:** crisis whipsaws and managed devaluations (ARS, KZT, TRY, ISK in 2008, CHF on 15 Jan 2015, March 2020). That is the cost of catching a quiet bad print like BRL 2000. They are for a person to look at, not to block on.
- **A bad print is mostly caught the night after it arrives,** when the next day takes it back: BRL 2000-02-11 is flagged one day late. Catching it on arrival needs the looser setting, which roughly doubles the soft flags.
- **Spikes under 2% are mostly missed;** most are within a normal day's range. Stale runs are already refused by the existing validation (no repeated rates except the peg), so the stale rule is a second line.
- **The isolation forest adds little** beyond the rules (13 of the 89 flags, small moves in volatile weeks). It is kept as a cheap extra, and the sweep shows the totals without it.
